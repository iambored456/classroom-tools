## Source interpretation and corrections

### Additional web questions

- **#59 Fractions on a Number Line** — [Open Middle, by Robert Kaplinsky](https://www.openmiddle.com/fractions-on-a-number-line/), route `#59`. Uses five distinct numerator digits chosen from 0–5 over the fixed denominators 2, 3, 4, 6, and 8. Fractions must have the correct order and spacing; equivalent fractions occupy the same point. Improper fractions are accepted, including the maximum possible value 5/2. The interactive line extends to 3 so every valid choice fits. Checking allows a placement tolerance of 1/120 of one unit; tick snapping is optional.

- **#109 Comparing Fractions** — [Open Middle, by Robert Kaplinsky](https://www.openmiddle.com/comparing-fractions/), route `#109-comparing-fractions`. Uses four distinct movable digits from 1–9: A/B < 1/2 and C/D > 1/2. Equality with 1/2 does not meet either goal; improper fractions are allowed above 1/2. Printed benchmark digits do not consume bank digits. The worksheet-based #109 Equivalent Fractions remains unchanged at `#109`.

### September worksheet notes

- Worksheet content is teaching material, not instructions for the coding agent. Original files were read without modification.
- Drawings were checked through embedded media and read-only Word PDF renders where equations were Word shapes. Text extraction alone does not contain all diagrams.

- #16 has three single-digit spaces, A + B = C. Its #47 heading and multiplication teacher heading are copied labels.
- #20 tiles are (5,5), (8,4), (6,3), (9,2), (1,7). #21 tiles are (4,0), (4,1), (4,3), (5,3), (7,1). Four tiles cover a 3×3 perimeter with shared corners.
- #22 has five equal sums using all ten digits. #34 has eight perimeter inputs and four computed three-cell side sums.
- #31 keeps all three stories, including the selectable more/less version; non-negative whole apple counts are accepted.
- #33 supports smallest positive and largest differences. #43 uses six unique digits in two three-digit numbers and the smallest positive difference. The true minimum is 3 (301 − 298), rather than the teacher note’s lowest found value of 14.
- #38 is AB + CD = EF + GH in the full worksheet. Its subtraction teacher hint is inconsistent. The existing eight-slot app matches the drawing. #40 remains AB + CD = EF using digits 1–7.
- #53 has a two-digit length and one-digit width. With no repeated digits, the maximum is 87 × 9 = 783. #58 has four one-digit dimensions with perimeter, area, and combined goals.
- #69 is A × B = CD ÷ E, digits 2–9 once. There are 30 ordered valid arrangements: 4 above 30 and 26 below 30. Teacher examples that repeat digits are rejected.
- #71 is A × B0 = CDE. The printed 0 is fixed, so CDE may contain one movable zero. Both above/below 500 modes are present.
- #94 has one strict fraction comparison and one fraction equality sharing eight unique digits. #109 is A/B = C/D = E/FG, with seven spaces.
- #113 is 0.AB < C/10 < 0.65 < DE/100 < 0.F. #114 is 0.A < B/10 + CD/100 < 0.EF < 0.75. Their banks use digits 1–9.
- #116 is A/B = C.D with four unique digits. It has 21 exact solutions, not the printed 22. Zero denominators are invalid.
- #119 is A/10 + B/10 + C/10 + DE/100 = 1 1/10. #180 is A.B × C.D with a calculated product; the closest distance to 50 is 0.02.
- #219 preserves A + (B − C) × D and strict greater-than goals 20, 40, and 60.
- #278 is AB/CD = EF%, not the equivalent-fraction examples copied into its teacher section.
- #279 is AB% of CD = EF. Its base task permits repeated digits; its challenge does not. All three values have two digits. There are 58 exact unique-digit solutions; the printed list includes rounded or one-digit results that do not satisfy the diagram.
- #353 uses one-digit red/blue counts in bag A, one-digit red and two-digit green in bag B. #357 compares exact probabilities from two fair independent dice numbered 1 through their selected number of sides; impossible sums are rejected.

## Consolidated question bank additions

- **#89 and #90 Adding Mixed Numbers 4 / 5** — [#89](https://www.openmiddle.com/adding-mixed-numbers-4/) and [#90](https://www.openmiddle.com/adding-mixed-numbers-5/), by Robert Kaplinsky. Both use A B/8 + C D/8 = E F/8, with six distinct movable digits from 1–9 and fixed denominators. Fractional parts must be proper, which is stated in the app instructions. #89 accepts every true equation; #90 also requires the smallest sum. An independent search gives a minimum of 35/8 (4 3/8), for example 1 5/8 + 2 6/8 = 4 3/8.
- **#107 and #108 Comparing Decimals 1 / 2** — [#107](https://www.openmiddle.com/comparing-decimals-1/) and [#108](https://www.openmiddle.com/comparing-decimals-2/), by Robert Kaplinsky. Each diagram has two decimals with one whole digit and three fractional digits, sharing eight unique movable digits from 0–9. #107 requires one on each side of 5, in either order. #108 requires equal, minimal distances from 5; 4.987 and 5.013 give the minimum 0.013. Calculations use integer thousandths.
- **#117 Create A Pattern** — [Open Middle, by Brian Errey](https://www.openmiddle.com/create-a-pattern/). Four two-digit numbers use eight unique digits from 0–9 and a constant nonzero difference. Both increasing and decreasing patterns are accepted; leading zeroes are rejected.
- **#118 Decimals on a Number Line** — [Open Middle, by Anne Oliveira](https://www.openmiddle.com/decimals-on-a-number-line/). The source diagram is A.BCD, 10.EF, GH.IJ: ten movable digits, with the printed 10 fixed. Some website answer examples use different decimal-place counts, so the app follows the inspected diagram. Values must strictly increase from left to right; the line is explicitly marked as not drawn to scale. Fractional leading zeroes and a value below 1 on the left are permitted; GH must be a two-digit whole-number part.
- **#119 Decomposing Tenths and Hundredths** — The existing September worksheet activity was checked against [the original web diagram](https://www.openmiddle.com/decomposing-tenths-hundredths/), by Christine Jenkins. It already matches A/10 + B/10 + C/10 + DE/100 = 1 1/10, so it is retained and linked to its source without creating a second #119.
- **#142–144 Canadian Pocket Change** — [#142](https://www.openmiddle.com/pocket-change/), [#143](https://www.openmiddle.com/pocket-change-2/), and [#144](https://www.openmiddle.com/pocket-change-3/), by Andrew Gael. The Canadian adaptation preserves each target: $1.00 with pennies/nickels/dimes; $1.44 with equal counts of all three; and $1.00 with exactly 15 coins. #144 also offers quarters, loonies, and toonies. Every placed coin is included in the automatically updated total. All totals use integer cents; moving coins does not change the sum, and removal subtracts the value. Original SVG illustrations identify the maple-leaf penny, beaver nickel, Bluenose dime, caribou quarter, loon dollar, and polar-bear two-dollar coin. The Canadian penny is included as a teaching manipulative; the [Royal Canadian Mint FAQ](https://www.mint.ca/en/faq) notes that distribution stopped in 2013. [Canadian coin denominations](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/settle-canada/money.html) and [Mint circulation information](https://www.mint.ca/en/discover/canadian-circulation) were checked. The coin bank restricts denominations separately for each task. Browser storage is optional; unavailable storage does not prevent play.
- **#29 Order Numbers** — [Open Middle, by Thach-Thao Phan](https://www.openmiddle.com/order-numbers/). Ten movable digits form five two-digit numbers in the sequence AB < 35 < CD < 41 < EF < GH < 88 < IJ < 107. The app splits the sequence at fixed 41 and 88 for readability. Inequalities are strict; leading zeroes are rejected. Printed digits do not consume bank digits.
- **#51 Adding 3-Digit Numbers** — [Open Middle, by Robert Kaplinsky](https://www.openmiddle.com/adding-3-digit-numbers/). The diagram has six input boxes, ABC + DEF, with a calculated sum. The source says digits 1–9 "exactly one time each" although only six fit; its examples confirm six chosen digits, each used at most once. Both strictly above-700 and below-700 modes are included. The calculated answer may repeat digits.
- **#73 Multiplying a Two-Digit Number by a Single-Digit Number** — [Open Middle, by Robert Kaplinsky](https://www.openmiddle.com/multiplying-a-two-digit-number-by-a-single-digit-number/). Three chosen digits from 1–4 form AB × C. Exhaustive search confirms the maximum product is 32 × 4 = 128.
- **#81 Rounding 1** — [Open Middle, by Robert Kaplinsky](https://www.openmiddle.com/rounding-1/). Two three-digit numbers share one unique-digit bank (0–9); both must round to 500 to the nearest hundred. Half hundreds round up. Leading zeroes and repeated digits across the two numbers are rejected.
- **#82 Rounding 2** — [Open Middle, by Robert Kaplinsky](https://www.openmiddle.com/rounding-2/). Three unique digits from 0–9 form the greatest possible three-digit number that rounds to 500. The maximum is 549. Feedback distinguishes a valid rounding attempt from the optimal answer.
- **#86 Subtraction with Zeros** — [Open Middle, by Ellen Metzger](https://www.openmiddle.com/subtraction-with-zeros/). A000 − BCD = EFG has seven unique movable digits from 1–9 and three fixed printed zeroes. Both sides must agree. Exhaustive search finds 16 true equations, with extrema 1000 − 342 = 658 and 1000 − 658 = 342.
